## Editorial approach

- Audience: people practising French for TEF Canada, including learners in and
  outside Canada. Use Canadian and Quebec settings without implying every
  Canadian shares a particular custom. Do not frame the videos as immigration
  advice or guarantee a score.
- Mix speaking and listening rather than producing only seasonal skits.
  Section A speaking asks for information; Section B involves arguing to
  convince. Listening is multiple choice. These short exercises are
  independent practice, not complete exam simulations or official questions.
  [TEF] The label `B1` in the vegetarian-cooking book exercise is an exercise
  number, **not** a CEFR level.
- **Proposed cadence:** start with one video per week, alternating listening and
  speaking when approved material permits. Treat seasonal episodes as that
  week's video, not extra obligations. Review workload and performance after
  four releases; no cadence, posting time or time zone is approved yet.
- Titles in the tables are working titles, not final on-screen text. Include
  `TEF Canada`, the skill and the task in the final title/caption. Use an
  original scenario or verify the source-book exercise and its reuse rights
  before adapting it. Fictional offers and announcements must not be presented
  as real event information.
- All new previews and production videos must be strictly under 90 seconds,
  including the spoken, measured word-highlighted title, pauses and book CTA.
  For listening, allow five seconds to read the choices, with a separate
  response countdown; the viewer may pause. These timings do not represent the
  official exam.
- Use available assets and the existing exam room; do not assume an event logo,
  mascot, sound, recording or book image is cleared for commercial use.
  Listening uses distinct Quebec French narrator and source voices plus
  appropriate context-specific sound, with human review of audio, accent,
  pronunciation and effects. Verify reference-voice and generated-output
  rights before release.

## Per-video release checklist

1. Select the exercise and record whether it is original fiction or a
   book-based adaptation; confirm permissions and the correct book CTA.
2. Verify a fresh matching preview when content, timing, voice or source
   changes. Check its actual encoded title highlights, five-second listening
   reading period where applicable, captions, scene and under-90-second clock.
3. Listen to the complete audio; review pronunciation, accent, effects, ASR
   disagreements and alignment warnings. Check mobile placement and rights for
   voices, generated speech, book material, images, props and sounds.
4. Obtain explicit preview approval before production; verify the final
   production and obtain separate publication approval. Keep both post drafts,
   independent-practice and advertising/AI disclosures and required credits.
5. Record the approved master, platform-specific caption, scheduled date/time
   and time zone, status, actual publication date and link. Until then use
   **unconfirmed**, not published. No scheduling service is connected by this
   plan.

After four actual releases, compare completion, retention, saves, comments and
book-link engagement if available. Record results with collection dates and
platforms; do not assume performance data or recommend a cadence as proven.
